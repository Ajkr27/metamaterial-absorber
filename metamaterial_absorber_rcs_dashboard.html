📋 Final Scientific Report — RCS Reduction Analysis Summary
Optimal Parameter Configuration

The configuration p = 13, m = 6 achieves a peak absorptance of 99.87% at 9.162 GHz (X-band). This near-unity absorption confirms destructive interference between electric and magnetic resonances, characteristic of an impedance-matched metamaterial absorber with Z ≈ Z₀.

Frequency Band with Highest Absorption

The X-band (8–12 GHz) consistently yields the highest peak absorptances across multiple p-m combinations. High-absorption regions (A > 90%) span from 5.4 GHz to 18 GHz, indicating broadband utility in microwave stealth applications including target detection and tracking radar systems.

RCS Reduction Interpretation

An absorptance of 99.87% corresponds to a scattering suppression of approximately −29.4 dB RCS reduction. This level of absorption effectively eliminates backscatter, rendering the target electromagnetically invisible to X-band radar. The metamaterial structure redirects energy into resistive losses within the unit cell.

Effect of Parameter p (Patch Size)

Increasing p from 11→14 shifts the resonant frequency downward (from 10.6 GHz to 6.6 GHz), following the effective medium relationship λ_res ∝ √(ε_eff · μ_eff). Larger patch dimensions increase effective capacitance, redshifting the resonance. This provides a design handle for targeting specific radar bands.

Effect of Parameter m (Gap Width)

Varying m at fixed p=13 modulates the inter-patch coupling and resonance quality factor. The optimal m=6 balances bandwidth and peak height. Smaller gaps (m=3) shift resonance to Ku-band (15.97 GHz); larger gaps (m=8) broaden absorption but reduce peak to 91.3%, indicating a bandwidth–efficiency tradeoff.

Engineering Significance

This metamaterial absorber demonstrates feasibility for low-observable (stealth) technology applications in defence platforms. The near-unity X-band absorption eliminates the need for bulky RAM coatings. The tuneable resonance via p and m enables multi-band or wideband absorber designs, aligning with IEEE Std 1502 methodology for RCS measurement and analysis.
